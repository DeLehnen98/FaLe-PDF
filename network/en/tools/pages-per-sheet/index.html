Several pages on one sheet

Save paper: print slides, notes and handouts with several pages per sheet – with a preview.

  • Stays on your device
  • No limits
  • No watermark
  • No account
How can I check that nothing is uploaded?

Your files are processed in this browser tab. This page has no function that sends files to a server, and its security rules (Content Security Policy) forbid connections to other servers.

Check it yourself: load this page, then switch on flight mode or disconnect Wi-Fi and run the tool. It still works – because everything happens on your device.

Good to know

How it works – step by step

  1. Choose your PDF

    Select the PDF, for example lecture notes or a presentation.

  2. Pick the layout

    Choose how many pages go on a sheet, the sheet size and the order. The preview shows every finished sheet.

  3. Arrange and print

    Tap “Arrange pages”, download the PDF and print it as usual.

Why FaLe?

  • As large as possible

    “Automatic” picks portrait or landscape so the pages come out as large as possible – usually landscape for slides, portrait for text pages.

  • Sharp, not pixelated

    Pages are placed as vector graphics. Text stays crisp and searchable even when scaled down.

  • Your order

    By rows or by columns, right to left, too. With a page range such as “1-10, 15” you decide which pages appear in which order.

  • Rotated pages done right

    Pages that are saved rotated in the original appear the way you see them on screen.

Frequently asked questions
Are my files uploaded?

No. The pages are arranged entirely in your browser. Your PDF never leaves your device.

Does the text stay searchable?

Yes. The pages are not turned into images but placed as scaled-down vector content.

Do links still work in the result?

No, links and form fields of the original pages are no longer clickable on the arranged sheet. For printing that makes no difference.

How do I print a booklet to fold?

Use the “Booklet” tool. It arranges the pages so you only need to fold and staple the sheets.

What about password-protected PDFs?

You unlock them right on the file. The result is protected again with the same password.
