Scan a QR code

Read QR codes with your camera, from a screenshot or a PDF – and see where a link really goes before you open it.

  • Stays on your device
  • No limits
  • No watermark
  • No account
How can I check that nothing is uploaded?

Your files are processed in this browser tab. This page has no function that sends files to a server, and its security rules (Content Security Policy) forbid connections to other servers.

Check it yourself: load this page, then switch on flight mode or disconnect Wi-Fi and run the tool. It still works – because everything happens on your device.

Good to know

How it works – step by step

  1. Start the camera or choose an image

    Tap “Start camera” and allow access – or choose a photo, screenshot or PDF with the code. Dragging a file in or pasting with Ctrl+V works too.

  2. Hold the code in view

    Keep the code steady in the frame. The light helps in the dark, zoom helps with small codes. A short beep tells you the code was read.

  3. Check and decide

    You see what the code contains: a link with its real destination and warning signs, Wi-Fi access, a contact, an event or a bank transfer. Nothing opens, copies or saves until you tap.

Why FaLe?

  • Protection from QR scams

    Links never open automatically. You see the real domain and warning signs such as short links, look-alike letters, disguised addresses or app downloads.

  • Understands the content

    Wi-Fi password to copy, contact as a file, event for your calendar, payment code with a checked IBAN – instead of cryptic text.

  • No app, no ads

    Runs in your browser, even offline after the first visit. Nothing is uploaded; the history stays on your device and can be cleared at any time.

  • Also from images and PDFs

    Codes on invoices, in screenshots or scanned letters are read straight from the file – handy when the code is shown on the same phone.

Frequently asked questions
Can I scan a QR code without an app?

Yes. This page uses the camera right in your browser. If your browser doesn’t allow camera access, take a photo of the code or choose an image – the result is the same.

How do I recognise a dangerous QR code?

Look at the domain right before the ending, such as “example.com”. Warning signs are short links, unusual endings, letters from other alphabets, an “@” in the address and links that download an app. This page shows you all of that before opening – it is still no guarantee.

What is quishing?

Quishing is fraud with QR codes: fake codes on parking meters, chargers, posters or in letters lead to imitation sites that steal logins or payments. So always check the destination and look out for codes stuck over the real ones.

Are my scans stored or sent anywhere?

Nothing is sent: the camera picture and files are analysed on your device only. The history lives in this browser only and can be switched off and cleared. Two-factor keys are never stored.

Can I read a QR code from a screenshot or a PDF?

Yes. Choose the image or PDF, drag the file onto the page or paste a screenshot with Ctrl+V. In PDFs every page is searched – several codes on one page, too.

Which codes are recognised?

QR codes always, also rotated, at an angle or light on dark. If your browser has a built-in barcode reader, the page also reads barcodes such as EAN and Code 128 as well as Data Matrix codes.
