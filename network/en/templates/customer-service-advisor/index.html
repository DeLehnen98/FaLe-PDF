What employers look for

Contact centres look for people who stay friendly under pressure, explain things clearly and work quickly across several systems. Many hire career changers and train them; expect a phone screening or a short role play.

  • Calm, friendly manner with frustrated customers

  • Clear spoken and written English

  • Quick, accurate work on a PC with several systems open

  • Flexibility for shifts or weekends

  • A quiet space and reliable internet for remote roles

Tips for your application

Do

  • Give one example of turning an unhappy customer around.
  • Mention metrics if you have them.
  • State your availability and home set-up for remote roles.

Don’t

  • Describe customers as annoying.
  • Send an application with typos.
  • Miss the screening call – keep your phone on.
Typical interview questions

Prepare a short, honest example from your own work for each one.

  1. A customer is shouting at you. What do you do?
  2. How would you explain a price rise to an elderly customer?
  3. How do you stay focused after many calls in a row?
  4. Which shifts can you work?

Salary expectations

If the advert asks for your salary expectations, give an annual gross figure or a range you have researched – through pay scales, salary surveys from public sources or people who do the job. If it does not ask, leave pay out of the letter; it will come up in the interview.

Frequently asked questions
Do I need experience for a customer service job?

Not always. Many employers train new advisors. Show examples from other jobs where you dealt with people, solved problems and kept calm.

Should I put a photo on my CV?

It depends on the country. In the UK, Ireland and the US a photo is usually left out to avoid bias, and many employers prefer CVs without one. In Germany and some other countries it is common but optional. If you use one: recent, friendly, plain background, dressed as you would be on a good day at work.

Can I copy the example letters?

Yes, you can use them. But recruiters read a lot of letters – one with your own examples, numbers and a real reason for choosing this employer will always do better. Use the examples as scaffolding and write the sentences yourself.

Is my data stored anywhere?

No. The CV and the letter are created in your browser. Nothing is stored unless you switch on “Save the draft” in the editor – and then only on your device.
