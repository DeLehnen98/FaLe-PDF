What employers look for

Practices want people who greet patients warmly, keep appointments running, handle confidential information carefully and – for assistant roles – support with clinical tasks such as blood tests or ECGs where permitted.

  • Experience on a busy front desk or in healthcare

  • Appointment systems and practice software

  • Confidentiality and data protection

  • Calm handling of phone, queue and requests at the same time

  • Clinical skills where the role includes them

Tips for your application

Do

  • Mention systems and tasks you handle.
  • Show calm under pressure with an example.
  • State hours and start date.

Don’t

  • Share identifiable patient stories.
  • Overstate clinical skills.
  • Send a generic letter.

Typical interview questions

Prepare a short, honest example from your own work for each one.

  1. How would you deal with an angry patient at the desk?
  2. Which systems do you know?
  3. How do you prioritise phone and desk?
  4. What does confidentiality mean to you?

Salary expectations

If the advert asks for your salary expectations, give an annual gross figure or a range you have researched – through pay scales, salary surveys from public sources or people who do the job. If it does not ask, leave pay out of the letter; it will come up in the interview.

Frequently asked questions

Is a cover letter expected for receptionist roles?

Usually yes. A short letter that shows you know the practice and can handle a busy desk helps a lot.

Should I put a photo on my CV?

It depends on the country. In the UK, Ireland and the US a photo is usually left out to avoid bias, and many employers prefer CVs without one. In Germany and some other countries it is common but optional. If you use one: recent, friendly, plain background, dressed as you would be on a good day at work.

Can I copy the example letters?

Yes, they are free to use. But recruiters read a lot of letters – one with your own examples, numbers and a real reason for choosing this employer will always do better. Use the examples as scaffolding and write the sentences yourself.

Is my data stored anywhere?

No. The CV and the letter are created in your browser. Nothing is stored unless you switch on “Save the draft” in the editor – and then only on your device.
